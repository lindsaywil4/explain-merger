Section: Discrepancies

Where the story and the paperwork part ways

Every claim on this page comes from documents UP produced or letters filed on the public docket. We show the records side by side and let you judge. Three patterns stand out.

Pattern 1: Support built before the facts existed

The public narrative

Roughly 2,000 letters from officials, businesses, and civic groups endorsed the merger as a win for local economies, the environment, and Texans.

The record

Bloomberg found the letters relied on a handful of templates circulated by a UP public relations firm before the application was filed. Signers could not have reviewed a transaction that did not yet exist on paper. The previously touted $750 million in concessions was quietly withdrawn.

Bloomberg, Dec. 29, 2025; UP-NS application support letters, Docket FD 36873. Full breakdown on the Before the Filing page.
What UP told the Texas Legislature

UP's Senior Director of Public Affairs testified in April 2025 that UP's average train length is 7,500 feet.

What UP told its investors

UP's 2025 SEC disclosures report quarterly average train lengths of 9,490, 9,689, 9,801, and 9,729 feet. Every quarter ran roughly 30% longer than the figure given to legislators.

UP testimony, Texas House Transportation Committee, Apr. 3, 2025; UP quarterly earnings reports / SEC filings, 2025.

Pattern 2: The review that misses the problem

UP's environmental review analyzed 68 grade crossings in the Houston region. Cross-reference those 68 against the FRA's blocked-crossing complaint database and the mismatch is stark.

25,196 FRA blocked-crossing reports at UP crossings in the 9-county Houston region, across 652 distinct crossings.
1.5% Share of those reports (371) that occurred at any of the 68 crossings UP analyzed.
0 of 20 Top-20 most-reported Houston crossings included in UP's review. All 20 are East End and Fifth Ward crossings, led by Eastwood St (2,905 reports), Leeland (1,441), and Milby (1,383).
#28 Rank of the most-reported crossing UP did analyze: Pineland Rd, on Houston's far northeast edge, with 122 reports. Only 12 of the 68 analyzed crossings sit inside Houston at all.

How did the worst crossings end up outside the review?

Thresholds. Because Houston is a severe ozone nonattainment area, environmental review of a rail segment triggers when merger-related traffic rises by 3 trains per day or 20% in gross tonnage. UP's filings assign the three approach segments east of Tower 87 exactly +5.00 trains per day, so they get analyzed. Every urban-core segment through the East End gets +1.88 to +2.29 trains per day, just under the 3.0 trigger, so the crossings on them, including all of the national top 20, fall outside the review. One San Antonio segment lands at +2.98, two hundredths below the line. The uniform, round increments and the near-miss pattern are exactly what community comments are asking the Board to make UP explain.

49 C.F.R. § 1105.7(e)(5); STB filings EI-34223, EI-34224, EI-34228, Docket FD 36873.
The Englewood Yard contradiction

UP's own Appendix Q, filed with the Board, lists Englewood Yard among manifest yards with a 20%+ activity increase: 2,044 to 2,456 cars per day, a 20.1% jump. The environmental review's yard table lists no Houston yard at all. The difference comes from splitting growth into "organic" and "merger-related" buckets, which leaves the merger share just under the 20% review trigger. Both numbers cannot drive the analysis at once. Community comments ask the Board to reconcile them.

UP-NS Appendix Q, STB filing 311221; environmental A3 yard table, EI-34224 series, Docket FD 36873.

Pattern 3: Houston's numbers shrink with every filing

UP has now given the Board three versions of its Houston projections. Each version is smaller than the last, and the changes control which impacts get reviewed and mitigated.

Metric Original (Dec 19, 2025) Amended (Apr 30, 2026) Errata (Jul 27, 2026)
Settegast added trucks/day +516 (246→762) +360 (242→614) +353 (→607)
Lafayette Sub (MP 353–327.7) +36.7% +5.00 trains/day unchanged; tonnage trimmed
Houston Sub at Tower 87 +36.8% +5.00 trains/day unchanged; tonnage trimmed
STB filings EI-34153 & EI-34157 (original); EI-34224 & EI-34228 (amended); EI-34262–63 incl. fn.7 (errata), Docket FD 36873.

The July errata trimmed merger carloads on the Beaumont, Glidden, and Lafayette segments too. Its cover letter never mentions Houston. The Settegast truck reduction appears only in footnote 7. Two months earlier, the railroads had assured the Board that not a single carload of their diversion study had changed. Maybe every revision has an innocent explanation. The point of the public comment process is to make UP provide one, on the record.

STB filings EI-34262–63; UP-62/NS-60 reply, May 26, 2026, Docket FD 36873.

Why residents should care about accounting fights. These are not paperwork technicalities. The traffic numbers decide whether the East End's crossings get studied, whether Settegast's truck routes get reviewed, and whether Houston qualifies for binding mitigation. Smaller numbers on paper mean smaller obligations later, whatever the trains do in real life.
