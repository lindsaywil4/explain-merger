Section: Take Action

Your experience is evidence. Put it on the record.

The STB is required to weigh community impacts. It can only weigh what reaches the docket. Three ways to help, ranked from five minutes to an evening.

1. Share your train story

Missed a shift because of a stopped train? Watched kids climb through railcars? Waited behind a blocked crossing with an ambulance? Tell us what happened. We compile resident stories and submit them to the STB docket on your behalf, so your experience counts even if you never file a thing yourself.

Share your story

2. File a comment with the STB

Anyone can file. You do not need a lawyer. Reference Docket No. FD 36873, say who you are and where you live, and describe how rail operations affect you. Specifics beat slogans: dates, streets, durations, what it cost you. Ask for what you want, whether that is denial of the merger or binding mitigation.

File at stb.gov

3. Show up when hearings are announced

Houston officials have formally asked the Board to hold a public hearing here, in evening or weekend hours. During the last big merger, the only regional hearing happened in Beaumont on a weekday. If the Board grants a Houston hearing, packed rooms matter. Watch this page and notanotherhouston.com for dates.

What makes a comment effective

A simple structure that works

Who you are. Name, neighborhood, and your connection to the rail corridor. "I live two blocks from the Eastwood Street crossing" carries weight.

What you experience. Concrete incidents with dates and durations. The FRA data shows the pattern; your story makes it real.

What you are asking for. Examples officials have already requested: a Houston public hearing, a full system impact study including secondary lines, environmental review of the East End crossings and Settegast truck routes, grade separation funding, quiet zones, and enforceable limits on train length and idling in residential areas.

Requests drawn from City of Houston comment, Oct. 13, 2025, and Commissioner Adrian Garcia comment (filing 310429).
Also worth doing: report every blocked crossing

Report blockages to the FRA portal at fra.dot.gov/blockedcrossings every time they happen. The crossing ID is posted on the blue sign at each crossing. This data built the national record that now anchors Houston's case. Underreporting shrinks the record; consistent reporting grows it.

For organizations and elected officials

Consider filing a Notice of Intent to Participate to become a party of record. It is not a yes or no vote on the merger. It reserves your seat at the table to request conditions and mitigation later. Several Houston-area offices have already filed. If your organization signed a pre-application support letter without board review, you can also file a comment clarifying or withdrawing your position.

STB Docket FD 36873 procedural schedule; participation records, 2026.

One last thing. This process runs on months and years, and railroads count on public attention fading. It faded in 1996, and Houston paid for it. Bookmark this site, tell a neighbor, and when the hearing date comes, bring them with you.
