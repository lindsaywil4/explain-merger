Section: The Opposition

This is not a partisan fight

Skepticism of the merger runs from House Republicans to progressive Democrats, from red-state attorneys general to big-city governments. They raise different concerns, but they converge on one demand: make the railroads prove it.

Republican members of Congress

On February 4, 2026, more than 40 Republican House members, including Houston-area Reps. Dan Crenshaw, Randy Weber, and Troy Nehls, wrote the STB. Their letter puts the burden of proof on the railroads: absent clear, measurable, and substantial benefits for manufacturers, farmers, energy producers, and consumers, the Board should not let the deal proceed. It recalls the backlash from the 1990s mergers and notes that industry consolidation has cut 30 Class I railroads down to six since 1980. It also cites the Trump Administration's focus on keeping costs down for American businesses and consumers.

Letter of 40+ Members of Congress to STB, Feb. 4, 2026, STB filing 310838, Docket FD 36873.
Which Texans signed the February letter?

Texas signers include Dan Crenshaw, Randy Weber, Troy Nehls, Michael Cloud, Beth Van Duyne, Jake Ellzey, Lance Gooden, August Pfluger, Pat Fallon, Ronny Jackson, John Carter, Craig Goldman, and Roger Williams. The full list spans farm states, energy states, and freight corridors nationwide.

STB filing 310838, signature pages.

Republican state attorneys general

On November 14, 2025, nine Republican attorneys general warned the Board that the merger risks undue market concentration, and with it higher prices, lower reliability, and less innovation for American manufacturers and consumers. Their letter argues high internal shipping costs would undercut American companies against foreign competitors, threaten agricultural producers, and could even compromise national security. The signers: Tennessee, Kansas, Florida, Mississippi, Iowa, Montana, North Dakota, South Dakota, and Ohio.

Letter of nine state attorneys general to STB, Nov. 14, 2025, STB filing 310163, Docket FD 36873.

The U.S. Senate and House caucuses

Senators John Hoeven (R-ND) and Amy Klobuchar (D-MN) wrote the Board on October 30, 2025, urging close scrutiny. On January 16, 2026, the House Monopoly Busters Caucus and Congressional Labor Caucus co-chairs sent their own letter, copying the Transportation Secretary, the FRA Administrator, and the head of the DOJ Antitrust Division.

Hoeven-Klobuchar letter to STB, Oct. 30, 2025; Monopoly Busters Caucus letter, Jan. 16, 2026, Docket FD 36873.

Cities and local governments

Chicago, the nation's largest rail hub, has pressed the Board on how the merger concentrates power over its terminal network, and its planning officials have filed detailed comments. Houston's mayor, county attorney, county commissioner, and council members have demanded hearings, system-wide studies, and binding mitigation. A Chicago-area oversight alliance filed environmental comments in February 2026. During the earlier CPKC merger, Chicago sought billions in mitigation and secured roughly $400 million, a precedent Houston advocates now cite.

Chicago Tribune coverage, Dec. 22, 2025 & Jan. 20, 2026; Chicago planning comment & Western Chicagoland alliance filing (EI-310853), Docket FD 36873; Houston filings 310158, 310323, 310413, 310428, 310429; community briefing records on CPKC mitigation.

Rival railroads and shippers

CPKC, CN, CSX, and BNSF challenged the completeness and integrity of the application itself. CPKC's December 2025 comments showed the railroads withheld the underlying traffic data behind their truck-to-rail projections, then directed anyone who wanted to check the math to buy it from S&P Global. S&P quoted CPKC $399,000 for the dataset. Trucking-side experts filed too: Atlantic Systems questioned where 2 million diverted truckloads would actually come from, and flagged the pollution hot spots the plan would create at urban terminals.

CPKC completeness comments, Dec. 29, 2025, STB filing 310605, at 8 & n.5; Atlantic Systems comment, Jan. 15, 2026, STB filing 310718, Docket FD 36873.
Why would other railroads oppose a merger?

Partly self-interest: a coast-to-coast UP-NS could squeeze rivals at shared gateways like Chicago, St. Louis, and New Orleans. But their filings also do the public a service. Their lawyers and economists audited the application line by line, and their objections about missing data and withheld agreements became the backbone of the STB's January rejection. The Board also flagged an "end-game" problem the railroads barely addressed: approving this deal invites a final round of consolidation. More on that next.

STB Decision, Jan. 16, 2026; CPKC filing 310605 at 17.
